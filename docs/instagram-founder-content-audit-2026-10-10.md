## Instagram plan built from the evidence

### Four editorial pillars

1. **NYC hospitality discovery — 40%.** Restaurants, hotels, rooftops and experiences. Lead with a useful reason to visit; include neighbourhood, occasion and one concrete detail.
2. **Fashion, beauty and wellness — 25%.** Personal-style stories and first-person trials. Protect Linda's creator voice and make disclosure obvious.
3. **Founder and creator business — 20%.** Behind-the-scenes briefs, production decisions, editing, creator economics and what Avenue One learned from the campaign.
4. **Culture and access — 15%.** Events, Broadway, Tribeca and New York moments that reinforce the taste-maker position.

### Weekly publishing system

- 3 Reels: one hospitality discovery, one fashion/beauty partnership or editorial, one founder/behind-the-scenes story
- 1 carousel: practical guide, saved list, campaign breakdown or “what made this work”
- Stories on active days: location context, production process, disclosure, poll/question and link
- 1 monthly founder-to-agency case note: public post on Linda's account, expanded explanation on the Avenue One founder page or blog, and an agency-channel recap

### Search and conversion improvements for captions

- Put the searchable phrase in the first sentence: for example, “NYC rooftop brunch,” “SoHo aesthetic clinic,” or “Brooklyn Mediterranean restaurant.”
- Use 3–6 specific tags rather than a long generic block: location + category + occasion + branded disclosure.
- Add one clear action: save for later, send to a friend, book, view the menu or read the agency breakdown.
- Credit production consistently. When Avenue One genuinely contributed, use a stable line such as “Creative direction / production: @avenueone.agency.”
- Preserve formal disclosures (`#Ad`, partner labels and offer-code terms) before decorative hashtags.
